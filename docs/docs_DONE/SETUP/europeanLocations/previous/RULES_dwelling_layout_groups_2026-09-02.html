FILTER

The ladder — eleven rules, first match wins

Order is the rule. A courtyard is tested before width, width before regularity, regularity before re‑entrance, and re‑entrance last by count of reflex vertices. Every test is dimensionless except the 8.0 m plate depth, which is a habitability constant rather than a local convention — that is what lets the ladder carry to countries the database has not reached yet.

COVERAGE

How far each group is from 95 %

“Real floor plan” means the building is cut into separate flats and a circulation core, floor by floor, and that partition survives into the energy model. A building without one is not skipped — it is simulated as a single empty box per floor, one thermal zone, no interior walls, no core. It still produces a number; that number just cannot describe flats. The goal is that 95 % of the 2,544 buildings carry a real plan. Today 541 do.

LAW

What is true on every floor plan, in every group

Nine clauses hold across all eleven groups, not one at a time. Six are coded, checked and capped — the owner fixed the last two numbers at CP-2, 2026-09-02. The last three, D-EU-73, D-EU-75 and D-EU-76, are not themselves checked: they constrain how the cutter draws, and D-EU-71/C9 and D-EU-72/C10 measure the result.

Already law, restated. Exactly one circulation zone per plate (SLIVER excepted, zero); flats and circulation cover at least 99.9 % of the plate; drawn dwellings equal claimed dwellings; every zone is a simple polygon — no hole, no zone inside another; at most 40 corner points per zone. D-EU-64.

D-EU-69 — every flat sees the outer façade. Owner, 2026-09-02: “every flat needs exposure to the sunlight and especially to the outer facade exposures, courtyard exposure is not enough.” At least 2.50 m of the flat’s boundary lies on the plate’s outer perimeter. A courtyard, a light‑well or any interior void does not count. Measured by C6, which reads footprint.exterior only.

Owner, 2026-09-02: “no need to sunlight exposure for corridors.” The corridor may be entirely interior — the second clause of D-EU-69.

D-EU-70 — the corridor reaches every flat. Owner, 2026-09-02: “corridor needs to touch to every flat zones.” The single circulation zone shares at least ACCESS_MIN_M (1.00 m) of boundary with every dwelling zone; no flat is reached through another flat. Measured by C8, promoted to a hard check in T03.

D-EU-71 — the corridor stays simple. One band of near‑constant width; no spurs, no blobs. Threshold fixed by the owner 2026-09-02 at CP-2: at most 16 corner points. Owner: “if possible it can be lower than that, because mostly corridors are simple shapes.” Not 12: the reference plate i_shape_linear_gallery (sheet 05, the corridor the owner praised) reads 15 corner points, so a cap of 12 would condemn the very shape the law is written to protect. Measured by C9. R2, the effective width 2·area / perimeter compared against CORRIDOR_W = 1.80 m, remains a reading only.

D-EU-72 — no narrow leftover space. No zone is a sliver too thin to inhabit. Threshold fixed by the owner 2026-09-02 at CP-2: no zone narrower than 2.00 m, measured as the largest width that still fits inside the zone. Measured by C10.

D-EU-73 — how a corridor is drawn. Stated by the owner 2026-09-02 from three annotated images (Screenshot_30.png, Screenshot_31.png, and a hand‑redrawn L_SHAPE/multi‑wing plate). In the first two a red box replaces a stepped, branched corridor with a single straight rectangle. In the third the owner redraws the whole plate: three flats, each one entire limb of the footprint with outer façade on three sides, and a short central red band at the waist where the limbs meet. Owner’s words: “red boundaries shows that we can propose simpler corridors”; “corridor can be central, the flat zones needs to be exposed to the outside facades, and simple design”; “the F2 proposal of yours was too complicated”.

(a) One limb, one flat. Each arm, wing or lobe of the footprint becomes one whole flat. A limb is never split lengthwise, and a flat never spans two limbs. This is what gives every flat outer façade on three sides (D-EU-69), and it is decided before any corridor is drawn.

(b) Central band, shortest that works. The circulation zone is one straight rectangle of constant width CORRIDOR_W placed at the plate’s waist — the junction where the limbs meet, or the mid‑span of a single limb — bearing chosen so it touches every flat (D-EU-70). It is sized to the minimum length that achieves that, never run end to end for its own sake. The core sits against it.

(c) No spur. The corridor never branches to reach a flat the band does not already touch. If a flat is out of reach, the band moves or changes bearing, or a flat boundary moves; a stub is never added.

(d) No step. The band’s two long edges are straight and parallel between its ends. A corner appears only where the outer boundary or a flat boundary clips an end.

(e) Chain, not tree. Where one band provably cannot touch every flat — a courtyard ring, four or more limbs — the circulation is a chain of straight bands joined end to end, each obeying (b)–(d). The joints are the only added corners. Never a T, never a cross, never a comb.

(f) Corner budget. 4 corner points per band, at most 2 more per clipped end, 4 more per additional band in the chain. This is what D-EU-71’s cap of 16 is rationing: one straight band costs 4–6, a two‑band chain 8–10, a four‑band courtyard ring 14–16.

(e) and (f) are superseded by D-EU-75, owner 2026-09-02. There is no chain and no ring: one plate, one band. Clauses (a)–(d) stand unchanged.

D-EU-73 is a cutter law, not a check: it says how the drawing is produced, where D-EU-71 and C9 only measure the result. It is written into this document by this plan (T07); the cutter itself is not touched. Reference cases: TEST_01 L_SHAPE example 2 (Bologna 29718), R1 = 23 today and 4–6 under (a)–(b); and the owner’s redrawn plate, where three limbs give three flats and one short waist band replaces a corridor that had been threaded through the middle of a flat.

D-EU-75 — one corridor, one rectangle, any bearing. Stated by the owner 2026-09-02 from four redrawn sheets — 01 Courtyard, 09 L shape, 10 U or T shape, 11 Complex multi‑wing: “if needed update the corridor rule, simple, centered, less point to generate corridors.” On every one of the four — a three‑wing plate, an L, a U and a courtyard ring alike — the owner draws the same object: one single straight rectangle, short, near the middle, at whatever bearing suits the plate. On 11 Complex multi‑wing the owner also draws the topology bare: three circles F1, F2, F3, and one circle marked corridor touching all three. That is the whole plan.

(a) Exactly one band, in every group. The circulation zone is a single straight rectangle for the whole plate, however many limbs the footprint has. This supersedes D-EU-73 (e). There is no chain, no ring, no second band and no gallery. Where one band cannot touch every flat, the flat boundaries move or the plate refuses — a second band is not the answer.

(b) Free bearing. The band is not bound to the plate’s axis, nor to any limb’s axis. Its bearing is chosen from the footprint’s own edge directions, and the bearing that touches every flat with the shortest band wins. The owner’s rectangles lie diagonally across the L and U plates for exactly this reason: the façade they follow is not the bounding box.

(c) Centred and short. Placed where the flats meet, sized to the minimum length that touches them all. D-EU-73 (b) unchanged — now with the bearing free instead of pinned.

(d) Corner budget, restated. One band is 4 corner points, at most 2 more per clipped end — 6 at most, against D-EU-71’s cap of 16. The 8–16 budget D-EU-73 (f) allowed for chains and rings is withdrawn with (a).

D-EU-76 — no absurd small space. Owner, 2026-09-02, on sheet 05 Corridor rectangle (Madrid way/435927693), ringing the stepped nubs along the corridor’s edge in red: “never create this kind of small absurd spaces, every flat zone single zone and if there are these kind of spaces insert to the closest space, corridor.”

(a) Every flat is one zone. One single polygon per dwelling. A flat that comes out of the cut in two or more pieces is not a flat: the largest piece is the flat, every other piece is a leftover.

(b) Every leftover is absorbed. A leftover is merged into the zone it shares the most boundary with — a flat, or the corridor. Nothing is left standing as a fragment and nothing is dropped: coverage stays at D-EU-64’s 99.9 %.

(c) Thin means leftover only when it is a fragment. A sliver narrower than D-EU-72’s 2.00 m is absorbed under (b). A whole flat that thin is a cut that failed, and is reported by C10 — never quietly merged away.

GLOBAL

Global rules — they hold in all eleven groups

These are not per‑group schemes. They bind every plate in the taxonomy at once, and a plan that breaks one is wrong whatever its group. The first three are the owner’s own statement of them; the rest were fixed earlier in the same arc and are listed with the check that measures each.

1 — Nothing narrower than 2 m. No zone anywhere in the plan may be thinner than 2.00 m — not a flat, not the corridor, not a leftover strip, in width or in length. A space below that is not a room; it is an artefact of the cut. Law D-EU-72, measured by check C10.

2 — The corridor is central, and it touches every flat. One circulation zone, placed where the flats meet rather than pushed to an edge, and every single flat opens onto it. A flat the corridor does not reach has no front door. Laws D-EU-70 and D-EU-73 (b), measured by check C8 at 1.00 m of contact.

3 — Every flat reaches the outer façade, for daylight. Each dwelling must hold a real length of the building’s outside wall. A courtyard façade does not count — an inner ring alone is not daylight. Law D-EU-69, measured by check C6 at 2.50 m of outer façade.

Also global, fixed earlier in this arc.

4 — Every flat is one single zone. One dwelling, one polygon. A flat that comes out of the cut in pieces is not a flat: the largest piece is the flat and every leftover is merged into the neighbour it shares the most boundary with — a flat, or the corridor. No small absurd spaces are ever left standing. Law D-EU-76, owner 2026‑09‑02; measured by checks C2 and C4.

5 — The corridor stays simple: at most 16 corner points. A circulation zone that needs more corners than that is a drawing failure, not a corridor. Law D-EU-71, measured by check C9.

6 — The plan fills the plate. Flats plus corridor plus cores cover at least 99.9 % of the footprint: no gaps, no overlaps, nothing dropped. Law D-EU-64, measured by check C1.

7 — Cores attach to the corridor, they never extend it. A stair or lift core hangs off the circulation zone; it may not be used to lengthen it or to reach a flat the corridor itself cannot reach. Law D-EU-67.

How to read a group sheet

Every sheet is one real building from that group — a representative, not a sketch, with its district and id in the corner. Drawing 1 is the plate as surveyed. Drawing 2 is the same plate after the group’s rule has cut it: each coloured cell is one flat and one thermal zone, the hatched cell is the circulation core, and the heavy outline is the original footprint, never modified. Both drawings were produced by the layout engine itself, not drawn by hand. Right of them is the rule in words: what defines the group, what the plate is, where circulation goes, how many zones a floor ends up with, and the named scheme. The line at the foot of each sheet is the refusal condition — the case the rule declines rather than guesses.

Each plan is cut at the group’s median flats per floor and repeats unchanged on every storey. Real buildings in the group carry more or fewer; the rule is the same, only the count changes. Above 8 flats on one floor the rule refuses outright.
