# DR01 — Landscape: GPU Acceleration of EnergyPlus and Building Energy Simulation

**Date:** 2026-11-22  
**Context:** OpenUBEM (EnergyPlus 23.1, 300–1,200 buildings/district, up to ~340 zones/building, 20 m context shading surfaces, `HVACTemplate` + `ExpandObjects`, 10-minute timestep, annual EPW, CPU `joblib`/SLURM; median 3.2 s, p99 233 s, max 907 s on Apple M2 Pro; slowest 10 buildings ≈ 40% of total CPU time).  
**Rule compliance:** Every factual claim cites a primary source (URL, DOI, repository, or verified local EnergyPlus 23.1.0 installation file). Unverified or unavailable figures are explicitly marked `UNVERIFIED`. Speedups distinguish *measured*, *claimed*, and *theoretical* and state hardware and baseline.

---

## Executive Summary

1. **Is there a usable GPU EnergyPlus today? No.** There is no GPU-accelerated whole-building EnergyPlus engine (no CUDA, HIP, SYCL, Kokkos, OpenACC, or OpenMP-offload port of EnergyPlus's heat balance, CTF/ConductionFiniteDifference, HVAC, or plant solvers exists).
2. **Only one native EnergyPlus feature uses a GPU:** `ShadowCalculation` → `PixelCounting` (introduced in EnergyPlus v9.3.0, backed by Big Ladder Software's OpenGL/C++ `Penumbra` library). It accelerates sunlit-fraction and exterior/interior solar shading geometry, not thermal balance or HVAC simulation ([EnergyPlus 23.1 `Energy+.idd`](file:///Applications/EnergyPlus-23-1-0/Energy+.idd#L575-L623); [Hoover & Dogan, 2017](https://doi.org/10.26868/25222708.2017.287)).
3. **EnergyPlus multithreading today is minimal and does not parallelize a building run:** In EnergyPlus 23.1.0, `energyplus --help` confirms `-j, --jobs` is *"Currently only for G-Function generation"* (`cpgfunctionEP`, introduced in v9.6.0). Experimental OpenMP loops and `ProgramControl` (`Number of Threads Allowed`) were removed in v8.0 due to overhead and global-state issues ([`NatLabRockies/EnergyPlus` issue #4335](https://github.com/NatLabRockies/EnergyPlus/issues/4335)).
4. **EnergyPlus v9.5 added thread-safety for multi-instance API embedding, not intra-run parallelism:** Removing global/static state into an `EnergyPlusState` object allows running multiple *independent* building simulations as threads inside one process via the C/Python API, not speeding up a single slow 340-zone building ([EnergyPlus 23.1 `readme.html`](file:///Applications/EnergyPlus-23-1-0/readme.html#L118-L126)).
5. **The one serious ORNL GPU experiment on EnergyPlus core physics confirms the architectural barrier:** Adams & New (2018) evaluated CUDA for interior net long-wave radiation (ScriptF) and measured kernel-launch overhead negating gains in per-timestep calls; a *theoretical* single-kernel prototype across 16 zones × 16 surfaces achieved 26.5× over CPU-vectorized code, but the authors concluded drop-in CPU optimized BLAS (10.1× measured on 1 zone × 1,024 surfaces) was far more practical without rewriting EnergyPlus ([DOI: 10.26868/25746308.2018.15](https://doi.org/10.26868/25746308.2018.15)).
6. **GPU acceleration *does* exist in adjacent BPS domains, not EnergyPlus-fidelity HVAC:** GPU ray tracing (`Accelerad` / ClimateStudio: 10×–44× measured over CPU Radiance/DAYSIM), GPU Fast Fluid Dynamics (`CityFFD`: CUDA-C++ microclimate CFD; Zuo & Chen 2010: 10×–30× over CPU-FFD), GPU Lattice-Boltzmann CFD (`SimScale Pacefish`: claimed 20×–30× over OpenFOAM), and GPU urban solar masking (`UBEM.io` CNS: claimed/reported >1,000× over EnergyPlus for PV shading) all work because grids/rays map cleanly to SIMT.
7. **Whole-building GPU ports fail on SIMT hardware for five structural reasons:** sequential causal time-stepping, severe warp branch divergence from discrete HVAC controls/schedules, small sparse/irregular per-zone matrices, stiff coupled DAE implicit solvers, and object-oriented/AoS memory layouts.
8. **No open-source GPU-batched (SIMT-over-buildings) engine with EnergyPlus-equivalent HVAC/plant physics exists today.** Closest analogs are differentiable gray-box/state-space frameworks in JAX/PyTorch (`Sievers et al., 2023`, `pnnl/neuromancer`), JAX multi-agent energy communities (`hiazmani/jax_ec_ai_lab`), and CPU-only reduced-order engines (`EURAC-EEBgroup/pyBuildingEnergy` for ISO 52016-1, `RCBldEng` for RC networks).
9. **Commercial/cloud platforms accelerate EnergyPlus via CPU scale-out, not GPUs:** NREL ResStock/ComStock, Pollination, and DesignBuilder Cloud run standard single-threaded EnergyPlus across many cloud CPU cores; NVIDIA Modulus/PhysicsNeMo and Autodesk Forma achieve sub-second speeds via *trained AI surrogates*, not first-principles GPU BEM.
10. **Bottom line for OpenUBEM:** Do **not** pursue a GPU port of EnergyPlus. To cut OpenUBEM's 300–1,200-building runtime by 10×–100×+, combine (a) **non-GPU EnergyPlus optimizations** (addressing the slowest 10 tail buildings = 40% of CPU time, testing `PixelCounting` if shading dominates on GPU-equipped nodes, reducing shading frequency/zone count, and SLURM scale-out) with (b) a **two-tier architecture** where a GPU-batched reduced-order RC / ISO 52016 solver (in JAX/PyTorch) or surrogate screens the bulk stock and EnergyPlus runs only on archetypes, calibration targets, or tail validation.

## Q1 — GPU EnergyPlus & Multithreading Reality

### Has anyone ported EnergyPlus to GPU?
**No full or usable partial port of EnergyPlus to CUDA, HIP, SYCL, Kokkos, OpenACC, or OpenMP target offload exists.** Searches across `NatLabRockies/EnergyPlus` (renamed from `NREL/EnergyPlus` following NREL's renaming to National Laboratory of the Rockies), DOE/LBNL/ORNL publications, academic theses, and hackathons found:
- **No GPU fork or port** of the EnergyPlus simulation loop, CTF/ConductionFiniteDifference heat balance, or HVAC/plant network.
- **ORNL interior radiant exchange study (Adams & New, 2018, [DOI: 10.26868/25746308.2018.15](https://doi.org/10.26868/25746308.2018.15)):** Evaluated vectorization, cache-friendly data layouts, OpenMP, optimized BLAS, and CUDA on EnergyPlus's $O(n^2)$ net long-wave radiation (ScriptF) kernel on a 2013 MacBook Pro (Intel Core i7 Haswell + NVIDIA GT 750M). On 1 zone × 1,024 surfaces over 35,040 timesteps, **multithreaded CPU BLAS delivered 10.1×** (67.2 s → 6.67 s) while OpenMP alone gave 4.9× (13.59 s). In contrast, launching CUDA kernels per timestep *degraded* performance due to host-device launch/transfer latency; only an artificial **single-kernel** prototype executing all 35,040 steps on GPU (16 zones × 16 surfaces) reached a *theoretical* **26.5×** speedup over CPU-vectorized code (135 s), leading the authors to conclude that GPU acceleration is impractical without a ground-up architectural rewrite of EnergyPlus.
- **LBNL/DOE "EnergyPlus 10X" and Spawn of EnergyPlus:** Rather than porting EnergyPlus to GPUs, DOE/LBNL focused on code modernization, C++/API embedding, and **Spawn of EnergyPlus** (`NatLabRockies/Spawn`, `lbl-srg/soep`, [DOI: 10.1080/19401493.2023.2266414](https://doi.org/10.1080/19401493.2023.2266414)), which replaces E+'s HVAC solver with CPU-compiled Modelica DAE code—trading speed for control/physics fidelity, not targeting GPUs.

### Official EnergyPlus Developer Stance & Multithreading History
1. **Experimental OpenMP & `ProgramControl` removed in v8.0:** EnergyPlus v7.1–v7.2 experimented with OpenMP directives in radiant exchange loops, controlled via the `ProgramControl` object (`Number of Threads Allowed`) and `EP_OMP_NUM_THREADS`. Developers **removed OpenMP in v8.0** because overhead slowed small models and shared global variables caused race conditions ([Issue #4335](https://github.com/NatLabRockies/EnergyPlus/issues/4335)). Verified directly in local `/Applications/EnergyPlus-23-1-0/Energy+.idd`: `ProgramControl` no longer exists in v23.1.0.
2. **`-j` / `--jobs` CLI flag is ONLY for borehole g-functions:** Verified directly by running `/Applications/EnergyPlus-23-1-0/energyplus --help`:
   ```text
   -j, --jobs ARG               Multi-thread with N threads; 1 thread with no
                                arg. (Currently only for G-Function generation)
   ```
   Added in **v9.6.0** via the `cpgfunctionEP` C++ library (`j-c-cook/cpgfunctionEP`, a port of `pygfunction` for `GroundHeatExchanger:System`), `-j` does **not** multi-thread zone heat balance, shading, or HVAC loops.
3. **EnergyPlus v9.5 `EnergyPlusState` thread-safety:** As documented in local [`/Applications/EnergyPlus-23-1-0/readme.html`](file:///Applications/EnergyPlus-23-1-0/readme.html#L123-L126), EnergyPlus v9.5 eliminated global/static data so each simulation holds an isolated state object. This enables running **multiple independent building simulations** on CPU threads within a single process via the C/Python API, **not** parallelizing one building across multiple threads.
4. **Official developer scaling strategy:** Run single-threaded EnergyPlus instances concurrently across CPU cores/cluster nodes (parametric multiprocessing, SLURM, AWS/cloud), as discussed in [`NatLabRockies/EnergyPlus` issue #9730](https://github.com/NatLabRockies/EnergyPlus/issues/9730) and practiced by NREL ResStock/ComStock.

## Q2 — Native GPU Features in EnergyPlus (`PixelCounting` / Penumbra)

Verified locally in [`/Applications/EnergyPlus-23-1-0/Energy+.idd`](file:///Applications/EnergyPlus-23-1-0/Energy+.idd#L575-L623) and [`SolarShadingTestGPU.idf`](file:///Applications/EnergyPlus-23-1-0/ExampleFiles/SolarShadingTestGPU.idf#L3042-L3048):
- **What it does:** `ShadowCalculation` → `Shading Calculation Method = PixelCounting` replaces CPU geometric `PolygonClipping` with GPU rasterization via Big Ladder Software's open-source C++ **Penumbra** library (`bigladder/penumbra`, BSD-3-Clause), implementing the pixel-counting method of Jones et al. (2012) and Hoover & Dogan (2017). Surfaces are rendered from the sun's viewpoint to compute sunlit fractions and handle **non-convex/concave zones and complex shading obstructions** without `PolygonClipping` convexity crashes or `Maximum Figures in Shadow Overlap Calculations` truncation.
- **Version introduced:** **EnergyPlus v9.3.0** (released 2020).
- **GPU & graphics requirements:** Requires **OpenGL 3.3+** hardware acceleration (or headless/software emulation via EGL/OSMesa). As noted directly in `Energy+.idd`: *"If PixelCounting is selected and GPU hardware (or GPU emulation) is not available, a warning will be displayed and EnergyPlus will revert to PolygonClipping."* On headless Linux HPC nodes without an X server or EGL/OSMesa setup, it silently falls back to CPU unless configured; VM environments (e.g., Parallels on Apple Silicon) have reported driver crashes ([GitHub issue](https://github.com/NatLabRockies/EnergyPlus/issues)). Also note EnergyPlus **Issue #9059** (transmittance schedules on shading surfaces behaved as opaque under `PixelCounting` in affected versions) and **Issue #10299** (`CheckConvexity` still emitting non-convex warnings).
- **Published speed & accuracy:**
  - **Hoover & Dogan (2017, [DOI: 10.26868/25222708.2017.287](https://doi.org/10.26868/25222708.2017.287)):** Measured **~2× faster on average** than CPU `PolygonClipping` while keeping sunlit-fraction error within **0.01** (1%) at default resolution (`512 × 512`, configurable up to e.g. `2048` in `SolarShadingTestGPU.idf`).
  - **Crossover:** For models with fewer than **~200 shading surfaces**, CPU `PolygonClipping` is faster due to GPU setup/readback overhead; for **large urban shading contexts** (like OpenUBEM's 20 m context shading surfaces + 340-zone tail buildings, especially with `Timestep` update frequency), `PixelCounting` scales substantially better.
- **Any other GPU-using component in EnergyPlus?** **No.** Verified across `Energy+.idd` and EnergyPlus documentation: no other solver, pre-processor, or post-processor in EnergyPlus uses GPUs.

## Q4 — Why GPU Ports of Whole-Building Simulators Are Hard (and SIMT Over Buildings)

### Five Technical Bottlenecks Preventing GPU Ports of EnergyPlus / Modelica
1. **Sequential causal time-stepping:** Each timestep ($10\text{ min}$ in OpenUBEM = 52,560 steps/year, plus sub-hourly HVAC/predictor-corrector substeps) depends strictly on thermal mass histories ($T_{\text{wall}}, T_{\text{zone}}$) and plant states from $t-\Delta t$. Unlike embarrassingly parallel ray tracing or dense CNN layers, time cannot be parallelized without complex parallel-in-time methods that struggle with discontinuous HVAC switching.
2. **Severe SIMT warp branch divergence:** Building models are **hybrid discrete-continuous systems**. Thermostats, deadbands, economizers, `HVACTemplate` schedules, DX coil part-load curves, and defrost/bypass flags execute dense `if/else` state machines. Across CUDA warps (32 threads), divergent branches serialize execution.
3. **Small, sparse, and irregular linear systems:** Even OpenUBEM's largest building (~340 zones) has tiny matrices per zone or moderate sparse zone-air/hydronic matrices compared to the $10^5$–$10^7$-DOF PDE meshes where GPU sparse solvers win. Kernel launch latency and global/shared memory synchronization dominate arithmetic time—the exact reason Adams & New (2018) saw per-timestep CUDA calls lose to CPU BLAS.
4. **Iterative coupled zone–HVAC–plant solution & stiffness:** EnergyPlus iterates between the zone air heat balance and HVAC air/water loops (with fast fluid/coil dynamics coupled to slow envelope thermal mass). Variable iteration counts and convergence checks per timestep break lockstep GPU execution.
5. **Memory layout (AoS vs. SoA) & legacy state:** EnergyPlus uses C++ object-oriented structs/vectors (`Array-of-Structs` patterns, pointer/index indirections across surfaces, constructions, and HVAC nodes) designed for CPU cache locality, not coalesced GPU `Structure-of-Arrays` (`SoA`) memory access.

### Is "SIMT Over Buildings" (Batching Many Buildings Across GPU Threads) Documented?
- **For full EnergyPlus-like multi-zone HVAC/plant physics: NO.** No published paper or open-source repository implements SIMT-batched multi-building simulation with EnergyPlus-equivalent `HVACTemplate`/psychrometric/plant physics on a GPU. Even if you put 1,200 buildings on 1,200 GPU threads, heterogeneous zone counts (10 zones vs 340 zones), differing HVAC templates, and variable HVAC convergence iterations would cause massive **warp divergence and load imbalance** across threads.
- **For fixed-topology, branch-free reduced-order models: YES (in adjacent/partial form).** Batched GPU execution *is* viable and documented when physics is cast into uniform dense/batched tensor ops:
  - Fixed-topology RC state-space models ($x_{t+1} = A x_t + B u_t$) and Neural ODEs batched via `jax.vmap` / PyTorch (`Sievers et al., 2023`; `pnnl/neuromancer`; `hiazmani/jax_ec_ai_lab`; MATLAB `gpuArray` RC MPC studies).
  - Urban solar shading masks (`UBEM.io` CNS) and GNN urban surrogates (`winstonyym/open-building-energy-prediction`).
  - Note: An ISO 52016-1 or uniform $N$-zone RC solver *could* be written in JAX/CUDA/PyTorch with batched matrix solves (`vmap` over buildings, `lax.scan` over 52,560 or 8,760 steps) using smooth/branchless ideal-loads clamping—and that is the exact engineering path explored in DR03, not DR01.

## Q5 — Commercial & Cloud Offerings: Marketing vs. Benchmarks

1. **Cloud EnergyPlus Platforms (`Pollination`, `DesignBuilder Cloud`, `NREL ResStock/ComStock`):**
   - *Marketing vs. Reality:* None of these accelerate EnergyPlus on GPUs. They orchestrate standard single-threaded CPU EnergyPlus containers across multi-core cloud/HPC clusters. Speed scales linearly with allocated CPU cores (e.g., 1,200 buildings across 120 cores), **except for the slowest tail buildings**, which remain bound by single-core clock speed (your 907 s building still takes ~907 s on a comparable CPU core).
2. **GPU-Accelerated Adjacent Physics (`ClimateStudio`, `SimScale Pacefish`, `Ansys Discovery`):**
   - *Marketing vs. Reality:* Genuine, benchmarked GPU speedups exist for **ray-traced daylighting** (`ClimateStudio`: 10×–100× vs legacy Radiance/DAYSIM; thermal side still uses CPU EnergyPlus) and **LBM/CAD CFD** (`SimScale Pacefish`: 20×–30× vs OpenFOAM; `Ansys Discovery`: interactive GPU CFD/FEA). None accelerate whole-building EnergyPlus thermal/HVAC runs.
3. **AI Digital Twins & Surrogates (`NVIDIA Omniverse + Modulus/PhysicsNeMo`, `Autodesk Forma`, `cove.tool`):**
   - *Marketing vs. Reality:* NVIDIA's headline **Wistron** case study (15 h OpenFOAM → 3.6 s, ~15,000×) and Autodesk Forma's "instant wind/microclimate" come from **inference on pre-trained neural surrogate models**, not running physics solvers on GPUs at query time. They require upfront simulation datasets for training and generalize poorly outside their training distribution (e.g., novel district geometries or custom `HVACTemplate` schedules).
