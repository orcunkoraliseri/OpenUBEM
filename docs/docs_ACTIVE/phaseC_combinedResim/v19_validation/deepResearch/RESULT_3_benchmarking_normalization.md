# Fair Comparison Methodology: Simulated EUI vs. NYC, LA, and Austin Benchmarking Data

This document establishes the methodology for performing an apples-to-apples comparison between the simulated Site Energy Use Intensity (EUI) generated by building energy models (e.g., EnergyPlus/OpenUBEM) and the measured energy benchmarking datasets from **New York City (Local Law 84/133)**, **Los Angeles (EBEWE)**, and **Austin (ECAD)**. 

To validate models accurately and avoid false conclusions (such as chasing artificial targets or misinterpreting the $+38.8\%$ over-prediction in Los Angeles), we must account for differences in EUI metrics, weather normalization, floor-area definitions, and property coverage thresholds.

---

## 1. New York City Local Law 84 (LL84) & Local Law 133 (LL133)

### 1.1. Reported EUI Metrics & Conversion Factors
* **Metrics Disclosed:** The public NYC Open Data benchmarking database discloses both actual (raw) and weather-normalized versions of both **Site EUI** and **Source EUI** (expressed in $\text{kBtu/ft}^2\text{/yr}$).
* **Conversion Basis:** Calculations are processed through the U.S. EPA's ENERGY STAR Portfolio Manager. Portfolio Manager converts site energy to source energy using national average source-to-site ratios. 
* **US EPA Source-to-Site Ratios:**
  * **Grid Electricity:** **2.80** (updated in 2018; previously **3.14** for filings prior to 2018).
  * **Natural Gas:** **1.05**.
  * **District Steam:** **1.20** (critical for NYC, where Con Edison operates the largest steam system in the US).
  * **District Chilled Water:** **0.91** (weighted average of electric chillers at 0.88 and gas chillers at 1.11).
  * **District Hot Water:** **1.20**.
  * **Fuel Oil (No. 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, Diesel):** **1.01**.
  * **On-site Solar/Wind Electricity:** **1.00**.

### 1.2. Weather Normalization Method
* **Method:** Portfolio Manager uses a location-specific linear regression model correlating monthly energy consumption with monthly Heating Degree Days (HDD) and Cooling Degree Days (CDD) calculated at a base temperature of **$65^\circ\text{F}$ ($18.3^\circ\text{C}$)**.
* **Weather Stations:** Daily temperature data is pulled from the nearest NOAA weather station (typically Central Park, LaGuardia Airport, or JFK Airport).
* **Normalization Target:** The regression coefficients are applied to local 30-year climate normals (currently utilizing the 1991–2020 normals) to calculate **Weather Normalized EUI**. This represents the expected energy use of the building under typical weather conditions.

### 1.3. Floor-Area Basis
* **Basis:** **Gross Floor Area (GFA)** as defined by ENERGY STAR Portfolio Manager.
* **Inclusions:** Total enclosed area measured between the outside surfaces of exterior walls. This includes conditioned and unconditioned basements, lobbies, mechanical equipment rooms, shafts, stairwells, and common areas.
* **Exclusions:** **Excludes all parking** (enclosed garages, open garages, and surface lots), exterior spaces (balconies, courtyards, patios), crawl spaces, unconditioned attics, and interstitial plenums.
* **Parking Correction:** If parking energy is metered together with the building (not sub-metered), the GFA of the parking must be entered as a separate "Parking" property use. Portfolio Manager then uses an empirical model to estimate and subtract parking energy consumption before calculating the building's EUI, ensuring the reported EUI represents only the non-parking building operations.

### 1.4. Property Coverage & Thresholds
* **Filing Thresholds:**
  * **2010–2016 Calendar Years:** Individual buildings **$\ge 50,000$ GFA**, or groups of buildings on a same tax lot or under a single condominium board **$\ge 100,000$ GFA**.
  * **2017 Calendar Year–Present (LL133):** Expanded to include mid-sized buildings **$\ge 25,000$ GFA** (often referred to as LL84/133). Groups of buildings on a single tax lot must comply if they exceed **$\ge 100,000$ GFA** in total.
* **Gaps:** Excludes properties under $25,000\text{ ft}^2$. Real estate sectors with high municipal/utility exemptions (such as heavy industrial facilities, manufacturing, and transit facilities) are largely missing or under-represented.

### 1.5. Reporting Vintage & Data-Quality Caveats
* **Vintage:** Public disclosures are available annually from **Calendar Year 2010** to the present.
* **Caveats:**
  * **No Mandatory Verification:** NYC does not require third-party professional verification (like a P.E. or R.A. stamp) for annual LL84 submissions. It is self-reported, leading to significant noise.
  * **Outliers:** The raw dataset contains severe outliers (e.g., EUI $< 1$ or $> 2,000\text{ kBtu/ft}^2\text{/yr}$).
  * **Unit Errors:** Owners frequently input incorrect utility units (e.g., entering natural gas in therms instead of ccf), inflating or deflating EUIs by factors of 10 or 100.
  * **Missing Tenant Data:** In multi-tenant commercial or residential buildings without master meters, landlords must request tenant data. If tenants refuse or data is omitted, the building reports an artificially low EUI (under-reporting bias).

## 2. Los Angeles Existing Buildings Energy & Water Efficiency (EBEWE)

### 2.1. Reported EUI Metrics & Conversion Factors
* **Metrics Disclosed:** The public LADBS EBEWE dataset reports both actual (raw) and weather-normalized **Site EUI** and **Source EUI** (expressed in $\text{kBtu/ft}^2\text{/yr}$).
* **Conversion Basis:** Uses ENERGY STAR Portfolio Manager. The source-to-site ratios are identical to the U.S. EPA defaults (Electricity: **2.80**, Natural Gas: **1.05**, Fuel Oil: **1.01**). District steam/chilled water options exist but are rarely utilized outside downtown LA.

### 2.2. Weather Normalization Method
* **Method:** Identical to the U.S. EPA Portfolio Manager regression methodology (monthly HDD and CDD base $65^\circ\text{F}$).
* **Weather Stations:** Climate data is correlated with the closest NOAA weather station (usually LAX Airport, Downtown USC, or Burbank Airport).
* **Normalization Target:** Normalizes energy use to local 30-year climate normals for the LA basin, accounting for the microclimates (coastal vs. inland).

### 2.3. Floor-Area Basis
* **Basis:** **Gross Floor Area (GFA)** as defined by Portfolio Manager (excluding parking). 
* **LA Caveat:** Due to the prevalence of large parking garages in Southern California, the parking exclusion is a major driver of GFA discrepancies. If parking is sub-metered, it is excluded; if not sub-metered, ESPM subtracts estimated parking energy.

### 2.4. Property Coverage & Thresholds
* **Filing Thresholds:**
  * Privately owned commercial and multifamily residential buildings **$\ge 20,000$ GFA**.
  * City-owned municipal buildings **$\ge 7,500$ GFA**.
  * Phased compliance schedule:
    * **2016 Calendar Year (reported 2017):** Buildings $\ge 100,000$ GFA.
    * **2017 Calendar Year (reported 2018):** Buildings $\ge 50,000$ GFA.
    * **2018 Calendar Year–Present (reported 2019+):** Buildings $\ge 20,000$ GFA.
* **Gaps:** Buildings $< 20,000$ GFA are entirely excluded. Datasets prior to 2018 are heavily biased towards very large commercial and multifamily properties due to the phase-in schedule.

### 2.5. Reporting Vintage & Data-Quality Caveats
* **Vintage:** Public disclosures are available annually from **Calendar Year 2016** to the present.
* **Caveats:**
  * **Mandatory Third-Party Verification:** To mitigate self-reporting errors, Los Angeles EBEWE mandates that building owners obtain **third-party verification of their benchmarking data every 3 years**. This must be certified by a licensed Professional Engineer (P.E.), Registered Architect (R.A.), or an approved credentialed professional. This makes the LA dataset significantly cleaner and more reliable than NYC's in its verified cohorts.
  * **California Assembly Bill 802 (AB 802):** Under California state law, utilities (LADWP and SoCalGas) are legally required to provide aggregated whole-building data to property owners upon request. This greatly reduces the "missing tenant data" gap that plagues NYC multifamily buildings, though data transmission and matching errors still occur.

## 3. Austin Energy Conservation Audit & Disclosure (ECAD)

### 3.1. Reported EUI Metrics & Conversion Factors
* **Metrics Disclosed:** Reports actual and weather-normalized **Site EUI** and **Source EUI** (expressed in $\text{kBtu/ft}^2\text{/yr}$).
* **Conversion Basis:** Uses ENERGY STAR Portfolio Manager defaults (Electricity: **2.80**, Natural Gas: **1.05**).

### 3.2. Weather Normalization Method
* **Method:** Standard ESPM monthly HDD and CDD regression model (base temperature $65^\circ\text{F}$).
* **Weather Stations:** Tied to local NOAA weather stations, typically Camp Mabry or Austin-Bergstrom International Airport.
* **Normalization Target:** Normalizes energy use to Austin's 30-year climate normals (characterized by very high cooling degree days and low heating degree days).

### 3.3. Floor-Area Basis
* **Basis:** **Gross Floor Area (GFA)**.
  * **Portfolio Manager Route:** Uses standard ESPM GFA rules (excluding parking, including basements/mechanical areas).
  * **Key Code Route:** Austin Energy pre-populates building data using GFA records sourced from the Travis Central Appraisal District (TCAD) and utility accounts. Property owners must verify this GFA. TCAD records sometimes represent Gross Building Area (GBA) which may include structured parking, introducing potential floor-area discrepancies.

### 3.4. Property Coverage & Thresholds
* **Filing Thresholds:**
  * **Commercial Buildings:** Annual benchmarking is mandatory for commercial buildings **$\ge 10,000$ GFA** that receive electricity from Austin Energy.
  * **Multifamily Buildings (5+ units):** **Exempt** from annual benchmarking. They are only required to undergo an energy audit every 10 years (starting when the building turns 10 years old) and disclose results to residents.
  * **Single-Family Residential (1–4 units):** **Exempt** from annual benchmarking. Audits are required only at the time of sale for homes $\ge 10$ years old.
* **Gaps:** The annual benchmarking dataset only covers commercial buildings $\ge 10,000$ GFA. **Multifamily and single-family residential properties are entirely absent from the annual EUI disclosure datasets.** Manufacturing facilities with a Predominant Use Tax Exemption are also exempt.

### 3.5. Reporting Vintage & Data-Quality Caveats
* **Vintage:** Commercial benchmarking data has been collected annually since **2012** (covering calendar year 2011/2012 onwards).
* **Caveats:**
  * **Pre-populated Utility Data:** Austin Energy's Key Code system allows utility data to be automatically uploaded, which eliminates manual utility bill data entry errors.
  * **No Third-Party Benchmarking Verification:** Standard annual benchmarking submissions do not require licensed professional sign-off (unlike the 10-year multifamily audits which must be done by a certified ECAD professional).
  * **TCAD Area Mismatches:** Because GFA is verified against tax appraisal records (TCAD), discrepancies in how appraisal districts measure square footage (e.g., inclusion of exterior corridors or parking garages in commercial buildings) can bias the calculated EUI.

## 5. Concrete Checklist of Corrections for Modelers

Before comparing simulated EUI (from EnergyPlus/OpenUBEM) against these city benchmarking datasets, apply the following corrections to ensure an apples-to-apples comparison:

### [ ] 1. Align Floor Area Inclusions and Exclusions
* **Action:** Exclude parking garages from the simulation’s floor area normalization.
  * *Discrepancy:* EnergyPlus models often include parking zones or structured parking. Because Portfolio Manager's GFA strictly **excludes parking**, including parking in the simulated EUI denominator will artificially deflate the simulated EUI.
  * *Correction:* Deduct the floor area and the energy consumption (lighting, ventilation) of any parking zones from the simulation results before computing EUI.
* **Action:** Include unconditioned zones (basements, mechanical rooms) in the simulated GFA.
  * *Discrepancy:* EnergyPlus models might exclude unconditioned basements or mechanical spaces from the floor area because they are not conditioned. However, Portfolio Manager **includes basements and mechanical spaces** in GFA.
  * *Correction:* Add the area of all enclosed basements and mechanical spaces to the simulated denominator to match the GFA basis.
* **Action:** Exclude plenum zones from the simulated area.
  * *Discrepancy:* EnergyPlus models often treat plenum spaces as active zones with floor area. GFA **excludes plenums**.
  * *Correction:* Ensure the floor area of plenum zones is not added to the EUI denominator.

### [ ] 2. Match Weather Normalization Type
* **Action:** If comparing against **Raw/Actual EUI** in a benchmarking dataset:
  * *Correction:* Run the EnergyPlus simulation using an **Actual Meteorological Year (AMY)** weather file for the exact year corresponding to the benchmarking data (e.g., use 2019 actual weather data to compare against 2019 benchmarking disclosures). Do not use TMY3.
* **Action:** If comparing against **Weather-Normalized EUI** in a benchmarking dataset:
  * *Correction:* Run the simulation using a **Typical Meteorological Year (TMY3)** weather file. Verify that the heating and cooling degree days (HDD/CDD base $65^\circ\text{F}$) in the TMY3 weather file are reasonably close ($\pm 5\%$) to the 30-year climate normals used by Portfolio Manager for that city. If they differ, adjust the simulated EUI using the simulated weather sensitivity.

### [ ] 3. Apply Exact Source Energy Conversion Factors
* **Action:** Do not use default EnergyPlus source energy multipliers.
  * *Discrepancy:* EnergyPlus default source-to-site ratios may represent outdated regional or site-specific grid mixes.
  * *Correction:* Multiply simulated site energy consumption by fuel type using the exact U.S. EPA Portfolio Manager source-site ratios:
    * Grid Electricity: **2.80** (or **3.14** if comparing to benchmarking vintages before 2018).
    - Natural Gas: **1.05**.
    - District Steam (NYC): **1.20**.
    - District Chilled Water: **0.91**.
    - District Hot Water: **1.20**.

### [ ] 4. Account for Dataset Coverage Bias
* **Action:** Apply size and property-type filters to simulated building portfolios.
  * *NYC LL84/133 Comparison:* Filter out any simulated buildings $< 25,000$ GFA.
  * *LA EBEWE Comparison:* Filter out any simulated buildings $< 20,000$ GFA.
  * *Austin ECAD Comparison:* **Do not compare simulated multifamily or residential buildings** to the annual benchmarking dataset, as Austin's annual benchmarking only covers commercial buildings. Filter out simulated commercial buildings $< 10,000$ GFA.

### [ ] 5. Clean the Measured Benchmarking Datasets (Data Quality Filters)
* **Action:** Clean the public datasets before establishing baseline targets. Apply these filtering rules:
  1. Remove records where `Gross Floor Area` $= 0$ or is blank.
  2. Remove records where `Site EUI` $= 0$, is blank, or is not available.
  3. Remove EUI outliers: Filter out buildings with `Site EUI` $< 5\text{ kBtu/ft}^2\text{/yr}$ or $> 500\text{ kBtu/ft}^2\text{/yr}$ (or $> 1000\text{ kBtu/ft}^2\text{/yr}$ for data centers/hospitals).
  4. Mismatched Area Filter: Remove buildings where the GFA reported in Portfolio Manager differs from the tax lot GFA (reported in PLUTO for NYC, or LADBS records for LA) by more than $\pm 20\%$.
  5. For LA EBEWE, prioritize benchmarking years 2020 and later to leverage the mandatory 3-year third-party verification cycle.
