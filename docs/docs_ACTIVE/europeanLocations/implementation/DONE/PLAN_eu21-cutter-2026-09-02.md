### `D-EU-74` — a corridor is an inscribed rectangle chain, never `band ∩ footprint`

*Director decision, 2026-09-02, derived from `D-EU-73` (d) "no step" and (f) "corner budget". Effective on
acceptance of this plan; the owner may overrule it, in which case T02 is redrawn and nothing downstream changes.*

Today every cutter builds circulation as a rectangle intersected with the plate:

```
band = box(cxmin, yc - 0.9, cxmax, yc + 0.9).intersection(P)           # 05_group_cutters.py:369
band = box(cxmin, maxy_local - 1.8, cxmax, maxy_local).intersection(P) # 05_group_cutters.py:375
```

`.intersection(P)` copies **every jog of the outer boundary that the band crosses** into the corridor outline. A plate
whose façade steps six times yields a corridor with six extra corner pairs. That single operation is the mechanism
behind 158 of the 173 failing plates. The law's answer is the opposite operation:

> **A circulation band is the largest rectangle of constant width `CORRIDOR_W`, on the chosen bearing, that fits
> *inside* the container — computed, not clipped. Its outline is 4 points. Where several bands are needed they are
> joined end to end into a chain (`D-EU-73` (e)); the joints are the only added corners.**

"Fits inside" is evaluated in the local frame: for a band of half-width `h = CORRIDOR_W/2` centred on axis value `c`,
the admissible span is the longest interval of the band axis over which the full-width strip stays within the
container. Bands are **shortened, never bent**, to achieve this. If no interval of length `≥ 2 · CORRIDOR_W` exists on
the chosen bearing, the bearing is wrong — try the perpendicular; if neither works, refuse with the existing token,
never emit a stepped band.

**Consequences pinned now, so the executor does not re-decide them:**

1. The rectifier is a **pure function of the intended band and the container**. It never looks at flats, `k`, or group.
2. Flats are always recomputed as `cell.difference(circ_rectified)` **after** rectification, never before — otherwise
   `C1` coverage breaks. This ordering is not negotiable.
3. Rectification may **shrink** the corridor. A flat that consequently gains area is correct, not a defect.
4. A rectified corridor that no longer touches a flat is a `D-EU-70` failure and must surface as `C8`, **not** be
   repaired with a connector spur. `D-EU-73` (c): *"a stub is never added."*
5. `_connector()` / `_bridge()` (`:627`, `:646`) produce mitred buffers around a nearest-points line — an arbitrary
   diagonal stub. Under `D-EU-73` (c) and (e) they may only ever be called to join two bands **end to end on a shared
   bearing**. Where they cannot, the plate refuses.

### T02 — `rectify_band()` — add it, wire nothing

**What.** Add one new function to `scripts/eu21/05_group_cutters.py`, near `_polygons_only` (`:620`):

```
def rectify_band(container, axis, centre, half_width, lo, hi): -> Polygon | None
```

It returns the **largest axis-aligned rectangle** of full width `2 · half_width`, centred on `centre` across `axis`,
whose span along `axis` lies inside `[lo, hi]` and which is **wholly contained in `container`**. Four corner points,
always. `None` when no admissible span of length `≥ 2 · CORRIDOR_W` exists.

**Why.** `D-EU-74`. This is the single geometric primitive the whole plan rests on: *computed, not clipped*.

**How.**
- Work entirely in the frame the caller passes; the function is frame-agnostic (`axis` is `"x"` or `"y"`).
- Admissibility of a span `[s, e]`: `container.contains(box(...))` for the full-width strip, tested at `0.001`
  precision — use `set_precision`, consistent with the rest of the file (`:646`).
- Find the longest admissible span by scanning the container's boundary coordinates along `axis` as candidate
  breakpoints, then bisecting each surviving interval to the tolerance already used in the file (`DENOISE_TOL`, `:39`)
  — no new constant, no new tolerance.
- Prefer, among equally long spans, the one whose midpoint is nearest the container centroid — `D-EU-73` (b),
  *central*.
- **Add the function only. Call it from nowhere.** No other line of the file changes.

**How to test.** Run `04_group_tests.py --test all` and the census control. The verdict counts must be **identical**
to T01's baseline: `321 / 173 / 56`. An unwired addition that moves a number means it was wired by accident. Also
self-test the primitive on three shapes and paste the six numbers into §8: a plain rectangle (span = full length), an
L (span stops at the notch), and a plate with a stepped façade (span stops at the first step).

### T03B — Solve for the band's line; chain a second band where one cannot reach

*Inserted 2026-09-02 by the owner's answer at `CP-1`: **(a)**, do not roll back. This task exists because T03 proved
`D-EU-74` but broke two §4 invariants — refusals 56 → 79, `C8` 5 → 23, 13 regressions — for one reason: `cut_convex`

**What.** Add two things to `05_group_cutters.py` and use them in `cut_convex`:

1. `solve_band(container, cells, axis_hint, centre_hint, half_width, lo, hi)` — chooses the bearing, the centre and
   the length of a single band, by calling `rectify_band` over a finite candidate set.
2. `chain_second_band(...)` — clause (e): one perpendicular band joined **end to end** to the first, used only when
   no single band touches every cell.

`rectify_band` (`:631`) is **not modified**. It stays the pure feasibility primitive `D-EU-74` (1) requires.

**Why.** `D-EU-73` (b) says the bearing is *"chosen so it touches every flat"* and the length is *"the minimum length
that achieves that"* — both are unknowns to solve for, not inputs to pin. `D-EU-73` (a) says the flats are
*"decided before any corridor is drawn"*, so the cells are available to solve against. T03's own text deferred the
bearing question by design; this is the plan meeting its own deferral.

**Director clarification of `D-EU-74` (1), pinned now.** "The rectifier is a pure function … it never looks at flats"
constrains `rectify_band` **only**. `solve_band` is a *chooser*, not the rectifier: it may read `cells`, because
`D-EU-73` (b)'s objective is stated in terms of the flats. `rectify_band` keeps its current signature and body.

**How — every choice is pinned here; the executor decides nothing.**

- **Cells before corridor.** In `cut_convex`, move the `cells` construction (currently at `:392`–`:400`, after the
  band) to **above** the band block. It depends only on `bx`, `miny`, `maxy`, `yc`, `odd_through`, `N`, `M` — never on
  `circ_local` — so the move is mechanical. `flats_local = [cell.difference(circ_local) …]` stays exactly where it is
  and keeps its ordering (`D-EU-74` (2), non-negotiable).
- **Candidate bearings**, in this order: `axis_hint`, then the perpendicular. Nothing else.
- **Candidate centres**, for a bearing: from the container's boundary coordinates on the *cross* axis (exterior ring
  plus every interior ring, the same collection `rectify_band` already does along its own axis), take for each value
  `v` the two centres `v + half_width` and `v - half_width`; add `centre_hint`; drop any centre whose full-width strip
  leaves the container's cross-axis bounds; deduplicate. Finite, deterministic, **no sampling grid**.
- **Selection**, in this order:
  1. Keep candidates for which `rectify_band` returns a rectangle.
  2. Keep those whose band **touches every cell** — `_touches(band, cell)` (`:733`) for all cells in `cells`.
  3. Among those, **shorten it**: trim each end inward by bisection at `DENOISE_TOL`, stopping while every cell is
     still touched. This is `D-EU-73` (b)'s *"minimum length that achieves that"*, and it cannot lose a contact by
     construction.
  4. Choose the **shortest** trimmed band; tie-break by centre nearest `centre_hint`, then by `axis_hint` bearing.
- **When no candidate touches every cell** — and only then — call `chain_second_band`:
  - Take the best single band (most cells touched; tie-break as above, untrimmed).
  - Second band bearing = **perpendicular** to the first.
  - Second band centre ∈ exactly **two** candidates: `s + half_width` and `e - half_width`, where `[s, e]` is the
    first band's span. This forces the joint to an **end**, so the result is an L. **A centre anywhere else would be a
    T or a cross and is forbidden by `D-EU-73` (e).** Do not widen this set.
  - `lo`/`hi` for the second band = the container's bounds on the perpendicular axis.
  - Accept the candidate whose union with the first is a **single polygon** and touches the most cells; tie-break
    shortest. Trim its free end by the same bisection.
  - If neither candidate improves the cell count, keep the single band.
- **Never a spur.** `_connector`/`_bridge` are not called from this path, at all. A band that still misses a flat is a
  `D-EU-70` failure and must surface as `C8` (`D-EU-74` (4), `D-EU-73` (c)).
- **Refusal.** No band on either bearing ⇒ `raise Refusal("BAND_LT_3M")`, the existing token. **Do not invent one.**
- `core_center` follows the chosen band: its centre stays on the band's centre line, at the midpoint of the band the
  core sits against. The core keeps its existing `.intersection(P)`.
- **Constants are untouched.** `CORRIDOR_W`, `CORE_*`, `LANDING`, `DENOISE_TOL`, `MAX_WING_PIECES` — none may move.
  So are `04_group_tests.py`'s thresholds. A check is never made easier to pass.

**How to test.** `.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts/eu21/04_group_tests.py --test all`, then the census control
against `outputs/eu_evidence/EU-21/rules_tests/baseline_2026-09-02/` — the **frozen T01 baseline, never regenerate

### T05 — The band must touch a flat the way `C8` measures it

*New task, written by the director 2026-09-02 after auditing T04. It occupies the T05 slot, which the
withdrawn courtyard-ring task left free. It is a **defect fix**, not a design change: `D-EU-75` is unaltered.*

**The defect, measured.** T04 landed `D-EU-75` and the corner budget worked — `C9` fell **158 -> 26**
(`COMPLEX_MULTI_WING` 45->6, `U_OR_T_SHAPE` 36->4, `L_SHAPE` 33->1, `COURTYARD` 15->3), refusals 56 -> 43,
`WING_TREE_FAILED` 17 -> 0, `C1` not-100 still 0. But **`C8` went 5 -> 114** and 33 plates regressed, including

`SQUARE` 2, `SLAB` 2.

**The root cause — one number, verified by the director.** The solver and the check do not measure the same
thing:

- the solver's full-touch test is `_touches` (`05_group_cutters.py:1032`) — `shared.length > 0.05`, i.e. **5 cm**

- `C8` (`04_group_tests.py:359`) is `min(sbuf(f, 0.05).intersection(circ).length) >= ACCESS_MIN_M` — **1.00 m**

showed. A free bearing makes hairline contacts feasible, so `solve_band` now happily returns a band that is

bearing with hairline contacts *beats* the axis-aligned band that had real contact. That is the whole of the
convex regression: 67 % of solved plates now sit at a non-zero bearing, including squares and triangles that have
no reason to.

**Why.** `D-EU-70` (corridor touches every flat) is a law; `C8` is its measurement. A cutter that satisfies the
