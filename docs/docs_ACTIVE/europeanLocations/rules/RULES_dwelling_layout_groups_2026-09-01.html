FILTER

The ladder — eleven rules, first match wins

Order is the rule. A courtyard is tested before width, width before regularity, regularity before re‑entrance, and re‑entrance last by count of reflex vertices. Every test is dimensionless except the 8.0 m plate depth, which is a habitability constant rather than a local convention — that is what lets the ladder carry to countries the database has not reached yet.

The last rule has no alternative below it: Complex multi‑wing is terminal and takes everything the ten above declined. No building can leave the ladder unclassified.

COVERAGE

How far each group is from 95 %

“Real floor plan” means the building is cut into separate flats and a circulation core, floor by floor, and that partition survives into the energy model. A building without one is not skipped — it is simulated as a single empty box per floor, one thermal zone, no interior walls, no core. It still produces a number; that number just cannot describe flats. The goal is that 95 % of the 2,544 buildings carry a real plan. Today 541 do.

The share with a real plan is nearly flat across the taxonomy — 9.9 % for Complex multi‑wing, 35.7 % for Slab, and every group in between. If shape alone decided the outcome the spread would be far wider. Recorded as FINDING 222: morphology is the second problem, not the first.

The first problem is FINDING 221. Roughly 939 buildings across the fleet compute a sound floor plan and then lose it, because the IDF writer demotes them to one massing box per floor when geomeppy’s clipper fails on its own intermediate geometry. Those plans exist; they are simply discarded. Recovering them is worth more than any new shape rule — on Lyon it moved the district from 35.4 % to 66.0 % on its own, without a single new scheme.

  4. 4
    Cut the flats
    • k sector cuts radiating from the void, equal area
    • every flat keeps its street facade
  5. 5
    Absorb what is left over
    • the wall between two flats is one straight line, wall to wall — the cut is drawn, not grown, so no dogleg is left where two claims met. A wall that genuinely bends round a wing is kept as it is
    • every leftover pocket goes to the flat that holds most of its wall, claimed in 0.20 m steps, so a margin running past two flats splits at the line between them instead of going whole to one
    • a ribbon along the core, under a metre wide, is folded into the core — but only if it does not widen the band: a block across the end of a corridor is narrow too, and folding it draws a wide head
    • a flat that comes out as two lobes joined by a neck gives the smaller lobe to the neighbour it shares the most wall with: a thermal zone is one room, not a room plus a tail
    • the outer wall is surveyed and is never redrawn — a jagged edge there is the building. Every cut and offset uses square corners, so no staircase of centimetre steps survives into the drawing
    • no zone encloses another. A flat that closes round the core, or a gallery that runs the whole way round a courtyard, is cut open and the piece handed to the neighbour that holds its wall: a zone floor is one outline, and neither a plan nor EnergyPlus can state a hole in it

How to read a group sheet

Every sheet is one real building from that group — a representative, not a sketch, with its district and id in the corner. Drawing 1 is the plate as surveyed. Drawing 2 is the same plate after the group’s rule has cut it: each coloured cell is one flat and one thermal zone, the hatched cell is the circulation core, and the heavy outline is the original footprint, never modified. Both drawings were produced by the layout engine itself, not drawn by hand. Right of them is the rule in words: what defines the group, what the plate is, where circulation goes, how many zones a floor ends up with, and the named scheme. The line at the foot of each sheet is the refusal condition — the case the rule declines rather than guesses.

Each plan is cut at the group’s median flats per floor and repeats unchanged on every storey. Real buildings in the group carry more or fewer; the rule is the same, only the count changes. Above 8 flats on one floor the rule refuses outright.

one flat = one thermal zone (heated) circulation core — stair/lift or 1.80 m corridor (unheated) heavy outline = the real footprint, unmodified scale bar = 10 m rule in force coded and running today scheme proposed written, not yet proven against the suite

What this is. The first step of the pipeline, written down: scan every building, sort it into a group, and only then assign a floor type. It replaces nothing — RULES_dwelling_layout_scheme_2026-08-28.html is frozen and still governs the two schemes it documents. The four new schemes named here (S1 courtyard perimeter band, S2 row-house depth bands, S3 wing spine decomposition, S4 regularized envelope grid) exist in openubem/geometry/european_residential.py but have not yet been proven against the test suite.

What this is not. Not an energy result. No EnergyPlus run stands behind any number on this page, and D-EU-55 forbids one without the owner’s own instruction. The counts are geometry and declared dwelling counts only.
