FILTER

The ladder — ten rules, first match wins

Order is the rule. A courtyard is tested before width, width before regularity, regularity before re‑entrance, and re‑entrance last by count of reflex vertices. Every test is dimensionless except the 8.0 m plate depth, which is a habitability constant rather than a local convention — that is what lets the ladder carry to countries the database has not reached yet.

COVERAGE

How far each group is from 95 %

“Real floor plan” means the building is cut into separate flats and a circulation core, floor by floor, and that partition survives into the energy model. A building without one is not skipped — it is simulated as a single empty box per floor, one thermal zone, no interior walls, no core. It still produces a number; that number just cannot describe flats. The goal is that 95 % of the 2,544 buildings carry a real plan. Today 541 do.

Roughly 939 buildings across the fleet compute a sound floor plan and then lose it, because the IDF writer demotes them to one massing box per floor when geomeppy’s clipper fails on its own intermediate geometry. Those plans exist; they are simply discarded. Recovering them is worth more than any new shape rule — on Lyon it moved the district from 35.4 % to 66.0 % on its own, without a single new scheme.

Filter

n_interior_rings ≥ 1
The footprint encloses at least one interior void. Tested first, because a ring plate cannot be cut by any grid that assumes a simply-connected plate.

Plate

A band of built depth wrapped around one or more open voids. Median plate 375 m² with a 23.5 m² void; the band, not the bounding box, is the plate that must be partitioned.

Circulation

One circulation zone per plate, and one only. It sits inside the plate, away from the outer wall. Any pocket a cut leaves over is grown into by whichever flat is closest, claimed in small steps so a margin that runs past two flats is split at the line equidistant between them rather than handed whole to one on an area tie-break. Once flats are settled, a sliver narrower than about a metre that still borders the core is folded into the core instead of staying a flat's narrow tail — a corner recess is usable room, not a staircase, but a thin neck against the core is circulation-shaped, not room-shaped. No square metre of the plate is left unassigned. Here that one zone is a 1.80 m deck-access gallery running right round the courtyard face, entered from one stair: every flat is reached off the gallery and still keeps its street facade, so the void becomes the access rather than dead space. Whatever the sector cuts leave over against the street wall is folded into the nearest flat, which is why no white gap remains beside F3. The engine today instead drops a small stair core at a sharp outer corner, which is why this group is redrawn under the proposal.

Thermal zones per floor

k dwelling zones cut along the band by equal area · exactly 1 circulation zone, the gallery. The void is not a zone and carries no surfaces. Plate coverage closes at 100 %: flats + gallery = footprint.

READ

How to read a group sheet

Every sheet is one real building from that group — a representative, not a sketch, with its district and id in the corner. Drawing 1 is the plate as surveyed. Drawing 2 is the same plate after the group’s rule has cut it: each coloured cell is one flat and one thermal zone, the hatched cell is the circulation core, and the heavy outline is the original footprint, never modified. Both drawings were produced by the layout engine itself, not drawn by hand. Right of them is the rule in words: what defines the group, what the plate is, where circulation goes, how many zones a floor ends up with, and the named scheme. The line at the foot of each sheet is the refusal condition — the case the rule declines rather than guesses.

Each plan is cut at the group’s median flats per floor and repeats unchanged on every storey. Real buildings in the group carry more or fewer; the rule is the same, only the count changes. Above 8 flats on one floor the rule refuses outright.
