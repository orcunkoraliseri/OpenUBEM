D‑EU‑36

Ruled in half — the bar is set, the core is not

Ruled 2026‑08‑28  Coverage bar: ≥ 95 % of buildings is enough. 100 % is not required.

So EU-13B fails closed on the residual: a building the ruled grid cannot express keeps one_zone_per_floor and is counted in a disclosed residual — it is never forced into a grid that does not fit it. What the bar does not permit is reducing a building’s declared dwelling count so that it fits, which is the defect itself.

Still open below, and T06 stays blocked on it: whether the core is carved out of the plate or added outside it.

The half still open: every plan on the right contains a grey hatched zone — a stair core or a corridor. Today the real-footprint path emits none, on any of the 4,186 buildings. That is not an oversight: under the D-EU-01/GEO-01 area-conservation contract an observed shell has no declared gross-to-conditioned relationship, so a core can only come from one of two places. You must pick which.

Recommended (a) Carve it out of the footprint

The circulation is taken from the observed plate, exactly as drawn here. The building keeps its real outline; heated floor area drops by the circulation share — 5.0–11.7 % across these eight. Every EUI denominator moves, so every published district figure moves with it.

Why: the core is the mechanism behind the party-wall buffering the MVP cites (bu = 0.50–0.80, 30–50 % transmission moderation). Without it the ruled model is not being run. The cost is a denominator change, which is disclosable; publish conditioned area beside gross footprint area on every building.

Alternative (b) Add it outside the footprint

What generate_external_unconditioned_core already does for the synthetic TABULA plate: the core is additional area bolted onto the shell, so A_C_Ref is untouched and no EUI denominator moves.

Why not: it makes the modelled building larger than the footprint that was measured, and the function refuses any non-rectangular plate — which is nearly every real building here. It states something false about the district in exchange for keeping a number stable.

Whichever you rule, EU-13B T06 stays blocked until you do, and T09 (resimulation) is a separate call — the plans below change zone geometry, so the published EUIs describe the old zoning either way.

The bar is met at fleet level, and it costs no coverage. The ruled scheme reaches more of the fleet than the current implementation does (97.05 % against 73.7 %) while emitting the correct dwelling count where the current one over-counts. The only failure mode anywhere is the > 8 per-storey refusal: zero habitability failures, zero area failures.

Always disclose  London is below the bar at 78.0 %. Its 82 prepared buildings carry 18 refusals, the densest of the four districts. The bar is a fleet bar — no London district-level result may be described as meeting it. Bologna’s 100 % is likewise a property of its archetype-imputed dwelling counts, which never exceed the 4×2 ceiling, and is not evidence that the grid fits Italy better than Spain.

New — finding 204  The MVP’s two circulation criteria contradict each other outside a narrow window, and most of the fleet is outside it.

§4.3 states circulation as 6–12 % of gross floor area and 12.0–25.0 m² per floor. Those hold together only for plates of 100.0–416.7 m² — below 100 m², 12 % of the plate is under 12 m²; above 416.7 m², 6 % is over 25 m². On the fleet: 1,602 of 2,544 (63.0 %) inside, 542 too small, 400 too large.

It is structural, not marginal: §4.2 requires a 1.80 m double-loaded spine for 3×2 and 4×2, and 1.80 m across a 55 m plate is 99 m² whatever §4.3 says. On the eight sheets below the percentage rule holds everywhere (5.0–11.7 %) while the 25 m² ceiling is exceeded on five of seven, up to 97.91 m². The prototype let the percentage win, reading 12.0–25.0 m² as descriptive of the Ankara plates it was measured on (mean 18.4 m²). Which criterion binds is yours to rule — the executor is instructed to emit the percentage, tag every departure, and report the count, never to resolve it.
