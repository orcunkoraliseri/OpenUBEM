FILTER

The ladder — eleven rules, first match wins

Order is the rule. A courtyard is tested before width, width before regularity, regularity before re‑entrance, and re‑entrance last by count of reflex vertices. Every test is dimensionless except the 8.0 m plate depth, which is a habitability constant rather than a local convention — that is what lets the ladder carry to countries the database has not reached yet.

COVERAGE

How far each group is from 95 %

“Real floor plan” means the building is cut into separate flats, floor by floor, and that partition survives into the energy model. Under the no‑core regime (D-EU-79) the whole plate belongs to flats, with nothing else drawn on it. A building without a real plan is not skipped — it is simulated as a single empty box per floor, one thermal zone, no interior walls. It still produces a number; that number just cannot describe flats. The goal is that 95 % of the 2,544 buildings carry a real plan. Today 541 do — this figure predates the no‑core regime and is carried forward unchanged; it counts a building as planned regardless of which regime drew it.

LAW

What is true on every floor plan, in every group

Six checks bind every plate at once, whatever its group, exactly as scripts/eu21/04_group_tests.py:312 (run_checks) implements them and scripts/eu21/07_nocore_tests.py runs them for the no‑core regime. The plain-language version of the same rules is the GLOBAL chapter below.

D-EU-79 — the no-core regime. A plate divides into dwellings only, with nothing else drawn on it and no rule, check or sheet here naming what used to sit between the flats. An earlier, more complex regime is parked with its own cutter intact; only the owner may restart it.

D-EU-80 — no empty space per floor. Every square metre of the floor plate belongs to exactly one flat. Any area left over by the division is absorbed into the flat it touches most — the closest flat — never left blank. Equal flat areas are not required and never justify leaving a gap. Check C1, coverage 0.999 ≤ cov ≤ 1.001.

D-EU-81 — nothing narrower than 2 m. No flat may contain any part narrower than 2.00 m. The width of a flat is the widest disc that fits inside it, measured exactly as the check measures it. Check C10, widest_fit ≥ 2.00 over flats only.

D-EU-69 — every flat sees the outer façade. At least 2.50 m of a flat’s boundary lies on the plate’s outer perimeter; a courtyard or light‑well does not count. Check C6, sbuf(f,0.05).intersection(fp.exterior).length ≥ 2.50.

D-EU-76 — every flat is one zone. One dwelling, one polygon: the largest piece is the flat, and every leftover merges into the zone it shares the most boundary with. No small absurd spaces are left standing. Checks C4 (one room per flat, largest pairwise overlap < 0.02 m²) and C5 (simple outline, no interior ring, no zone containing another, ≤ 40 points per zone).

Drawn equals claimed. The number of flats a plate draws is exactly the number of dwellings it declares. Check C3, len(flats) == n.

GLOBAL

Global rules — they hold in all eleven groups

These are not per‑group schemes. They bind every plate in the taxonomy at once, and a plan that breaks one is wrong whatever its group. All four are the owner’s own no‑core ruling of 2026‑09‑02, each with the check that measures it.

1 — A plate is divided into dwellings only. Nothing else is drawn on it, and no rule, check or sheet here names what used to sit between the flats. Law D-EU-79.

2 — No empty space per floor. Every square metre of the plate belongs to exactly one flat. Any area left over by the division is absorbed into the flat it touches most; equal flat areas are never required and never justify a gap. Law D-EU-80, measured by check C1 at 99.9–100.1 % coverage, and by check C3 (drawn flats equal claimed dwellings).

3 — Nothing narrower than 2 m. No flat may contain any part narrower than 2.00 m, measured as the widest disc that fits inside it. Law D-EU-81, measured by check C10.

4 — Every flat reaches the outer façade, for daylight. Each dwelling must hold a real length of the building’s outside wall. A courtyard façade does not count — an inner ring alone is not daylight. Law D-EU-69, measured by check C6 at 2.50 m of outer façade.

Also global, fixed earlier in this arc.

5 — Every flat is one single zone. One dwelling, one polygon. A flat that comes out of the cut in pieces is not a flat: the largest piece is the flat and every leftover is merged into the neighbour it shares the most boundary with. No small absurd spaces are ever left standing. Law D-EU-76, owner 2026‑09‑02; measured by checks C4 and C5.

How to read a group sheet

Every sheet is one real building from that group — a representative, not a sketch, with its district and id in the corner. Drawing 1 is the plate as surveyed. Drawing 2 is the same plate after cut_nocore has divided it: each coloured cell is one flat and one thermal zone, and the heavy outline is the original footprint, never modified. Both drawings were produced by the layout engine itself, not drawn by hand. Right of them is the rule in words: what defines the group, what the plate is, how many zones a floor ends up with, and the named scheme. D-EU-79 gives this regime no refusal path: every plate with a usable footprint is cut, and a plate that cannot clear a check is a FAIL, never an invented refusal.

Each plan is cut at the group’s median flats per floor and repeats unchanged on every storey. Real buildings in the group carry more or fewer; the rule is the same, only the count changes.

one flat = one thermal zone (heated) heavy outline = the real footprint, unmodified scale bar = 10 m rule in force coded and running today
FALLBACK

Generic Floor Plans for Uncatalogued Buildings (No-Census Fallback)

Real cadastral manifests (e.g. 02_residential_manifest.gpkg) often contain structures with no matching entry in the national housing or morphology census (garages, uncatalogued blocks, auxiliary annexes, or commercial ground-floors). While these buildings sit outside the formal 95 % evaluation denominator, urban scale workflows still require sensible interior thermal zoning rather than inert massing blocks.

Rule: Uncatalogued / Unknown Fallback Assignment. When a building has a surveyed footprint but no census row:

  • Morphology classification: The footprint geometry is measured (convex hull deficit, interior courtyards, aspect ratio, rectangularity, reflex vertices) and assigned to one of the 11 standard morphology groups above.
  • Dwelling density estimation: For Sliver buildings (width < 8.0 m), k = 1 flat per floor by definition. For all other groups, dwelling count per floor is estimated from surveyed footprint area at a standard European residential allowance (~70 m² per flat): k = clamp(round(area / 70.0), 1, 12).
  • Zoning generation: The equal-area direct cutter (cut_nocore) partitions the footprint into k dwellings without unconditioned cores or circulation zones (D-EU-79).
  • Status and visualization: These buildings are labeled as UNKNOWN / GENERIC_FALLBACK and rendered in Dark Green (rgb(24, 94, 46)) in 3D viewers to clearly distinguish estimated generic layouts from officially validated census buildings (bright green PASS).

What this is. The first step of the pipeline, written down: scan every building, sort it into a group, and only then divide it into flats. cut_nocore (scripts/eu21/07_nocore_tests.py, ported from 06_nocore_control.py:145) is the one cutter behind every group on this sheet: k equal-area columns in the plate’s own frame, clipped to the true footprint, with every leftover folded into the flat it touches most (D-EU-80). The earlier, per-group schemes this sheet once named are superseded and parked with their engine in openubem/geometry/european_residential.py; only the owner may restart that path.

What this is not. Not an energy result. No EnergyPlus run stands behind any number on this page, and D-EU-55 forbids one without the owner’s own instruction. The counts are geometry and declared dwelling counts only.
