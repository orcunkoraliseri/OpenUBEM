Where this stands, in plain language

  1. You picked the numbers that could already be wrong. That is four problems, and they do not share a fix. Two of them are about energy per square metre: we divide a building's energy by a floor area we assumed, and for most buildings the simulation was not actually run on that floor area. One is about the energy itself: every building's lights and equipment are modelled as if built to 2022 code, whatever year it was really built. The fourth is about a measuring instrument: the score that says how often the building-type guesser is right slid four points and nobody knows why. Grouping them was your call and it was the right one — they are the only items on the register that can make a published result wrong rather than merely incomplete.
  2. All the measuring is done, and the news on 6 August is good. The two big unfixed problems both live in one method of dividing a building into rooms — and that method cannot be picked by accident, only by name. I checked all 16,320 buildings behind our adopted results: not one used it. So the numbers this project stands on are not affected. The two problems are not smaller than they were; we now know where they stop, and it is short of anything we have published as a headline.
  3. Most of the decisions are now taken; two remain. Decided 9 August (CP-M3, with two others folded into it): a piece of work is not finished until it shows its accuracy score before and after, records the construction era it used, and repairs the links into anything it archives. All three obligatory. Also decided the same day: the halted fleet run restarts on the cluster, measured first rather than committed blind; the never-working failure check gets fixed everywhere rather than only where it was noticed; and you authorised the full pass, telling me to carry it through without coming back for permission. Still waiting on you: whether the cross-method comparisons are worth salvaging (CP-M2), and the classifier question below. Neither blocks the cluster work.
  4. One question from last week is still unanswered, and it is not in this plan. The classifier question (OPEN-22): when the map data says only "this is a building", the code guesses office. If the human answer key guessed office too, our accuracy score is measuring two guesses agreeing. It is carried at the bottom of this board as S03 so it cannot be lost again — but it is a separate, cheap job, and it does not block anything here.
  5. The rule that shapes this whole board: measure first, fix second. Several of these items rest on numbers written down weeks ago. One of them — OPEN-22's — was checked this week and its entire premise had silently become false: the code had changed underneath the note describing it. So no fix gets designed for anything here until its size has been measured against today's code. The measurement tasks are explicitly forbidden from fixing what they find.
