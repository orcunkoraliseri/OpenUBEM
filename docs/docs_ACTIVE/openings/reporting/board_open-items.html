2026-08-22, later — the fix was built, it was wrong, and the suite caught it

The correction to OPEN-61 was written and it broke three tests. Not three obscure ones — the three that check the total energy of the project’s reference buildings. That is the good outcome, and it is worth being plain about why: the error was in the instructions I wrote, not in the work that followed them.

What happened. The simulation output reports district heating as a column with fourteen rows — one per end use. The plan told the executor to read the total row and treat it as hot water. That is correct for every building in the fleet, where hot water is 100 % of district heating. It is exactly wrong for the project’s three reference buildings, which are the mirror image: 100 % of their district heating is space heating and none of it is hot water. So the fix quietly moved space heating into the hot-water bucket and inflated the total.

The diagnosis cost one database query and one division. The clue was that only the total failed — heating, cooling and lighting all still passed on the same buildings. That pattern means energy was added, not miscalculated. The reference building carries 148.24 GJ of district heating over 392 m², which is 105.0456 kWh/m²; the test reported a gap of 105.0454. Case closed without an investigation.

The lesson is bigger than the bug, so it is written down as a rule. A number measured over the 8,152-building fleet is evidence about the fleet. It is not evidence about the test suite — and the test suite is what gates every change. Nobody asked whether the test buildings resemble the real ones. They do not.

Then the fix was proven against real simulation output, not just against tests. 200 buildings drawn across all twelve cells and re-parsed from the preserved 121.9 GB corpus. Both testable conditions pass 200 of 200, with a median error of 1.3×10-16 — that is floating-point noise, i.e. exact. The third condition could not be tested at all: it needed a building with no district heating, and the sample population does not contain one. Recorded as untestable rather than as passed.

One thing in that validation deserves your attention and I am not going to bury it. When the 200 buildings were rebuilt from scratch, 158 of them came out with a different floor area than the census recorded. Mostly by less than a hundredth of a percent, but the worst is off by 15.8 %. Every tall building reproduced exactly; the drift is confined to the small and mid-rise ones. This did not affect the result above — scoring in absolute energy instead of energy-per-square-metre removes it entirely, which is how the 200/200 was obtained. The cause is now known, and it is not a model defect. The preserved corpus kept every building’s results database but only 799 of 7,861 of the companion files that record the simulator’s own floor area. Without it the rebuild falls back to footprint × number of storeys. The correspondence is total and I checked it myself rather than taking it on report: all 158 drifted buildings lack that file and all 42 exact ones have it — zero exceptions. So this is a gap in what was archived, not a building that changes size. I told you a moment ago this looked like OPEN-62; that was premature and I am withdrawing it. What survives is smaller but still real: the storey-count route disagrees with the simulator’s own area by up to 15.8 %, which is worth a ruling on its own terms.
