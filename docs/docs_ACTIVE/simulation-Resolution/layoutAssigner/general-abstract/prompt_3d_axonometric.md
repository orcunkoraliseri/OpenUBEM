# Image-generation prompt — LayoutAssigner 3D axonometric abstract

**Purpose:** a single realistic 3D axonometric illustration that tells the same pipeline story as the technical flowchart, but as an architectural visualization — for a title slide / cover image rather than a working diagram. Paste the prompt below into Gemini (or any image model) as-is.

**Tool:** Gemini web · **Style:** realistic 3D axonometric/isometric architectural rendering, not a flat diagram.

---

## Prompt to paste

Create a single wide realistic 3D axonometric (isometric) architectural illustration, in the style of a high-end architecture-firm portfolio rendering or a "building-tech" magazine cover — clean studio lighting, soft shadows, realistic materials (glass curtain wall, concrete, brushed metal, muted glazing), a light neutral background (very pale gray or off-white, no sky or landscape context), shown as one continuous horizontal composition made of five connected vignettes read left to right, each sitting on its own small floating ground plane, linked by thin elegant arrows or a subtle flowing ribbon/baseline connecting each ground plane to the next.

**Vignette 1 (leftmost):** an irregular, real-world city building footprint — an L-shaped or notched polygon slab, mid-rise, on a small urban block with a couple of neighboring generic low context buildings faded to translucent gray so this one building stays the focus. Give it a small floating label beneath: "real building".

**Vignette 2:** the same building now replaced by a clean, rectangular, standardized prototype tower — a crisp rectangular massing with regular window bands, clearly more "generic ASHRAE-standard" in character than vignette 1's irregular shape. Show a thin dashed ghost outline of the original irregular footprint from vignette 1 hovering faintly beneath/behind the new rectangular volume, plus a small scaling grid (a translucent floor grid with corner arrows pointing outward) to suggest the volume is being scaled up or down uniformly. Small floating label: "prototype substitution + area scaling".

**Vignette 3:** the same rectangular volume, now shown as an architectural cutaway/exploded axonometric — peel away the front facade to reveal one representative floor plate's interior zoning: a double-loaded corridor down the middle with rows of rectangular room modules on both sides (apartment-unit style layout), rendered in soft pastel zone-fill colors (e.g. warm beige for units, cool gray for the corridor) so the thermal-zone subdivision is legible. Above this representative floor, show 3-4 translucent ghost floor slabs stacked upward, fading in opacity toward the top, to suggest "this one floor repeats upward as identical stacked levels." Small floating label: "multi-zone floor plan × floor multiplier".

**Vignette 4:** a small weather/climate transition — split the vignette visually in two halves: left half shows a muted cold-toned mini skyline with a snowflake icon (representing the prototype's native Buffalo climate), right half shows a warm sunny mini skyline silhouette with a small sun icon (representing the real target city). A thin arrow crosses from the snowflake half to the sun half. Small floating label: "climate & weather-file handoff".

**Rightmost vignette 5:** the finished building volume (matching vignette 3's massing) rendered fully solid and polished, glowing very subtly at the window bands (warm interior light), connected by a thin arrow to a small stylized gear/chip icon labeled inside it with tiny abstract line-chart bars (representing simulation and results), and a small floating results panel (a minimalist card showing a tiny bar chart and a single glowing number, no real text needed, just an abstract "data readout" motif). Small floating label: "EnergyPlus simulation → results".

Overall composition: all five vignettes should share the same camera angle, scale, and lighting so the eye reads it as one continuous story rather than five unrelated images. Keep the palette restrained — soft neutrals plus one warm accent (amber/gold) used consistently across the arrows and the vignette-5 glow, so the accent color visually ties the whole sequence together. No text other than the five short floating labels described above; no additional logos, watermarks, or people.
