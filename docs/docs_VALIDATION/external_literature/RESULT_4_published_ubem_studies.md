## 1-Paragraph Synthesis of Consensus UBEM Accuracy

There is a clear consensus in published Urban Building Energy Modeling (UBEM) literature regarding a multi-tiered performance gap between building-level and district-scale validation: archetype-based UBEMs typically exhibit high errors at the **individual building level** but achieve high precision at the **aggregate stock level** due to error cancellation. For individual buildings, annual energy consumption predictions without detailed calibration typically suffer from error margins (MAPE or CV(RMSE)) of **40% to 100%** (Cerezo Davila et al., 2016; El Kontar & Rakha, 2019), which can be reduced to **14% to 26%** through Bayesian calibration, clustering, or automated smart-meter parameter tuning (Sokol et al., 2017; El Kontar & Rakha, 2019; Chen et al., 2020). Conversely, when these individual simulated profiles are aggregated to the district, cohort, or city scale, the stochastic variances of occupant behavior, infiltration, and internal equipment loads naturally offset and average out, yielding aggregate annual stock errors of **less than 1% to 10%** (NMBE < 5% and CV(RMSE) < 15%) (Sokol et al., 2017; Kristensen et al., 2018; Chen et al., 2020; Dogan et al., 2025). This establishes that a per-building deviation of ~45% is a standard, expected characteristic of archetype-based bottom-up models rather than a structural defect, provided that aggregate stock performance remains tightly aligned with measured benchmarks.
